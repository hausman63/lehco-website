Why we started

We started LEHCO because there's a Delco shirt, a Chesco shirt, a Bucks shirt, and nothing for the county that gave the world a bulldog on a hood and a bell in a basement. Lehigh County has stories nobody else gets to tell. We just figured somebody should put them on a hoodie.

Dave and family, LEHCO

The long stories

Every county has landmarks. Ours have plot twists. Nine to start with.

  • Mack TrucksBorn in Allentown in 1905, Mack put a snarling bulldog on the hood and Lehigh County on the map of American manufacturing.
  • The Liberty Bell's hideoutIn 1777 the Liberty Bell went underground, hidden beneath Zion's Reformed Church in Allentown for nine months.
  • A-Treat sodaBottled in Allentown since 1918, A-Treat is the taste of a Lehigh County summer, birch beer included.
  • Hess's department storeAllentown's legendary department store basically invented the mall food court, and a parade nobody who saw it ever forgot.
  • Shankweiler's Drive-InRunning in Orefield since 1934, Shankweiler's is America's oldest continuously operating drive-in theater.
  • Dorney ParkDropping stomachs and building memories since 1884, Dorney Park is Lehigh County's own amusement park institution.
  • The Trexlertown velodromeThe T-Town Velodrome turned a quiet corner of Lehigh County into one of America's most storied cycling tracks.
  • Lee IacoccaBorn and raised in Allentown, Iacocca went on to reinvent Ford's Mustang and save Chrysler from the brink.
  • Saquon BarkleyBefore he was breaking NFL tackles, Saquon was Whitehall's own, Lehigh County's homegrown star running back.
